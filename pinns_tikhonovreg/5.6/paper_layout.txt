                                           Abstract

         The Cauchy problem for multi-dimensional nonlinear elliptic equations is refor-
      mulated as a least squares problem and then stabilized by Tikhonov regularization
      method. The last is solved in the framework of artificial neural networks, while the
      regularization parameter is chosen by the discrepancy principle or the L-curve method.
      Numerical simulations show that the approach is efficient for this severely ill-posed,
      multi-dimensional and nonlinear problem.


Keywords: Cauchy problem, multi-dimensional nonlinear elliptic equation, ill-posed prob-
lems, regularization, L-curve method, artificial neural networks.



1     Introduction
The Cauchy problem for elliptic equations arises in many problems of Science, Technology,
Medicine, Physics and Geophysics [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]. The practical situation is that we have
access only to a part of the boundary of the domain where the physical process is monitored,
and we have to predict the process on the remaining (non-accessible) part of the boundary.
This problem is unfortunately ill-posed as Jacques Hadamard shown. Following Hadamard,
a problem is well-posed if 1) there exists a solution, 2) the solution is unique, and 3) the


                                                1
solution continuously depends on the data in an appropriate topology [7]. Once, at least one
of these conditions is violated we say that the problem is ill-posed.
In 1902 Hadamard [8] considered the Cauchy problem for the Laplace equation

if and only if

is analytic in a < x < b. Thus, not for every Cauchy data φ and ψ there exists a solution
to the Cauchy problem for the Laplace equation, and if there exists one, some compatibility

time, Hadamard though that ill-posed problems have no physical meaning. However, as
shown, for example, in [3, 4, 5, 6] many problems in Science and practice are ill-posed. In
1943, Tikhonov [9] realized that a problem is ill-posed due to the lack of information on
the solution and he introduced the notion of conditional well-posedness. In 1963, Tikhonov
[10] created the so-called Tikhonov regularization method to stabilize ill-posed problems and
since then the theory of ill-posed problems has been intensively developed [3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 11].
It seems that Lavrent’ev, Landis and Pucci are the first who studied the Cauchy problem
for elliptic equations from the viewpoint of ill-posed problems [13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20]
and up to now there have been many papers devoted to this problem; for surveys on them,
the reader can consult [4, 5, 6, 12, 21, 22] and the references therein. Further, we note that
in [23, 24] it was investigated the ill-posedness of Cauchy problems for elliptic equations
based on the analysis of the singular values of the continuation problem operator obtained
analytically. In [25], it was made numerical analysis of the Horizontally Diagonalize and
Fit (HDF) method and Landweber iteration for the 3D Cauchy problem for the Poisson
equation. HDF discretizes horizontal variables and transforms the 3D problem into a series
of decoupled 1D problems in the vertical dimension which significantly reduces the size of
problem. It was shown that the number of 1D problems is a regularization parameter.
Payne and his collaborators started studying the Cauchy problem for nonlinear elliptic equa-
tions (CPNlEE) in the sixties of the last century [26], [27], [28]–[34] and [35]. In this series of
work the authors proved several pointwise estimates for the solution of the Cauchy problem
based on the log-convexity method. It seems to us that the research on CPNlEE in the
last century is very moderate, we could not find other papers accept for the above ones. In
this century, Kügler and Leitão (2003)[36] seem to be the first to consider CPNlEE. There
they formulated CPNlEE as a variational problem or a fixed point problem and solved that
by Mean value iterations. This research has been also adapted in [37]. Ly and Tarkhanov
[38, 39, 40] investigated the problem from a rather theoretical point of view. The authors

                                                 2
proved some existence results and proposed some variational formulations for the problem.
Some results are presented by Kallel et al [41], Andrieux and Baranger [42]. So, the research
on CPNlEE is quite few. To our best knowledge there has been no global stability estimate
and stable numerical methods are quite sparse.
As mentioned above, although there have been many papers devoted to theoretical aspects
of the Cauchy problem for elliptic equations, those to numerical aspects of that for multi-
dimensional nonlinear problems are quite few. This paper is an attempt to stably solving the
Cauchy problem for multi-dimensional nonlinear elliptic equations. Namely, we reformulate
the Cauchy problem as a least squares problem and then stabilize it by Tikhonov regulariza-
tion method. We solve the last in the framework of artificial neural networks (ANN), while
the regularization parameter is chosen by the discrepancy principle or the L-curve method.
Numerical simulations show that the approach is efficient for this severely ill-posed, multi-
dimensional and nonlinear problem. We note that Li and Hu [43] have used ANN for solving
the Cauchy problem for elliptic and parabolic equations, however, when we implemented
their algorithms, the numerical solutions were not stable. We thought that solely ANN can-
not overcome the ill-posedness, therefore, in this paper we still use the standard Tikhonov
regularization method to stabilize the solution to the Cauchy problem, ANN are applied to
minimize the Tikhonov functional. So, ANN are only a tool for solving complicated non-
convex minimization problems arising in inverse problems, not a regularization method as
claimed in some previous reports.

4      Training algorithms for networks and regularization
       parameter choices
A typical algorithm used for training networks is ADAM. Indeed, the authors of [43] used
ADAM for implementing their method. However, when we applied ADAM to the Cauchy
problem for elliptic equations, it did not provide good, stable numerical results, especially
for 2D and 3D nonlinear elliptic equations. Therefore, in this note we will use the L-BFGS
method (Limited-memory Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno algorithm) [44].
This algorithm is a type of quasi-Newton method, which shows its efficiency and flexibility
in solving unconstrained optimization problems. With its limited storage structure, L-BFGS
is very suitable for optimizing neural network models, especially when the model has a large
number of parameters. Instead of calculating the full Hessian matrix like traditional Newton
methods, L-BFGS only needs to store and update approximations of the inverse Hessian
matrix to reduce the computational cost.
Let θ := (w, b) denote the vector of all trainable parameters of the network (weights and
biases). The update rule is given by

                                         θk+1 = θk − βHk ∇Jα (θk ),                               (4.1)

where ∇Jα (θk ) is the gradient of the cost functional Jα at the k-th iteration, β is a learning
coefficient, and Hk is the inverse Hessian approximation at step k. The matrix Hk is updated
according to the BFGS formula

                        Hk+1 = I − ρk sk ykT Hk I − ρk yk sTk + ρk sk sTk ,
                                                            
                                                                                            (4.2)

with
                           1
                  ρk =            ,   sk = θk+1 − θk ,       yk = ∇Jα (θk+1 ) − ∇Jα (θk ).        (4.3)
                         ykT sk

These formulas describe the process of updating the inverse Hessian approximation Hk based
on the history of the correction pairs (sk , yk ). This process approximates the curvature
information of the objective function and ensures that Hk remains positive definite. By
updating the parameter vector θ according to (4.1), the L-BFGS method efficiently optimizes
the objective function and adjusts the neural network parameters to achieve stable and
accurate results when solving the Cauchy problem.

The above process is run for a fix regularization parameter α. To define the ”right” reg-
ularization parameter which yields optimal error of the method, we apply the discrepancy
principle (a posteriori method) and the L-curve method for the Tikhonov functional (3.1),
see, e.g. [11], [45]). The discrepancy principle chooses the regularization parameter α in the
(2.6) such that
                                     ∥Luδα − hδ ∥2L2 (Ω) = τ δ,
where uδα is the solution to the optimization problem (3.1), τ > 1. In all our numerical tests,
we chose τ = 1.1.

principle. All numerical experiments are carried out using Python 3.10.

5.4    Numerical Results and Discussion

The numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework, which combines Ar-
tificial Neural Networks with Tikhonov regularization, the selection of the regularization
parameter α via the discrepancy principle or L-curve method, and optimization techniques
using the L-BFGS algorithm, yields highly accurate and stable results across a variety of
geometries and problem types (both linear and nonlinear) despite the presence of Gaussian
noise. Below, we provide a detailed analysis of the numerical examples, highlighting the
robustness and effectiveness of the method.
2D Laplace Equation with Smooth Boundary Data (Example 5.1): For the case of
a smooth boundary function, the L2 error at x2 = 1 is remarkably low, achieving 3.8 × 10−3
with 1% noise (Table 1), and gracefully increases to 0.181 at 7.5% noise. The comparison
between the exact and ANN solutions (Figure 1) shows near-perfect alignment, underscoring
that a compact ANN model (3 hidden layers with 7 neurons, tanh activation) combined with
Tikhonov regularization based on ∥∂n u∥2L2 (Γ2 ) is sufficient to produce high-quality boundary
approximations for smooth data.
2D Laplace Equation with Hat Function Boundary (Example 5.2): Despite reduced
smoothness, the ANN effectively captures the characteristic shape of the hat function, with
L2 errors at x2 = 1 of 0.02097 (1% noise) and 0.06349 (5% noise) (Table 2). The plots (Figure
2) confirm that the peak of the hat function is well-preserved, demonstrating the method’s
ability to maintain moderately non-smooth features under appropriate regularization.
2D Laplace Equation with Step Function Boundary (Example 5.3): For the chal-
lenging case where the boundary data does not belong to the Sobolev space H 1/2 , the ANN
achieves L2 errors of 0.08979 (1% noise) and 0.15312 (5% noise) at x2 = 1 (Table 3). The
step edges are reasonably reconstructed (Figure 3), indicating good performance given the
irregular nature of the target data.
2D Poisson Equation in a Circular Domain (Example 5.4): Across 1–5% noise levels,
the global L2 error ranges from 0.0185 to 0.0512 (Table 4), with smooth error distributions
(Figure 4). The flux-based regularization on Γ2 effectively stabilizes the Cauchy problem in
curved geometries.
3D Linear Poisson Equation (Example 5.5): In a cubic domain, the global L2 error
with 1% noise is 0.00917, and 0.01027 on the slice x3 = 1, with controlled growth up to 5%
noise (Table 5; Figures 5–6). This highlights the method’s scalability to 3D problems using
a modest network (4 hidden layers, 10 neurons, tanh).
2D Nonlinear Cauchy Problem (Example 5.6): Despite the nonlinear term q(u) and
Cauchy data, the L2 error remains low, from 0.0183 (1% noise) to 0.0528 (5% noise) (Table 6),

                                              26
with the slice at x2 = 0.5 closely matching the exact solution (Figure 8). This emphasizes
the advantage of combining regularization with L-curve/discrepancy-based α selection in
nonlinear settings.
Effect of Cauchy Data Length in a Circular Domain (Example 5.8): When Γ1
spans 3/4 of the boundary, the L2 error is 0.0421; reducing to 1/2 increases the error to
0.0753, and at 1/4, it rises sharply to 0.8912 (Table 7; Figure 9). These results align with
the theoretical ill-posedness of the Cauchy problem: less Cauchy data exacerbates instability,
yet the method yields reliable solutions for sufficiently large data portions (3/4 or 1/2).
3D Nonlinear Cauchy Problems (Examples 5.9, 5.10, 5.11): Across 3D domains,
including a smaller [0, 0.5]3 cube, the L2 errors range from 0.01693–0.05871 (Table 8) and
0.02138–0.04015 (Table 9) for 1–5% noise. In the smaller domain (Example 5.11), errors re-
main controlled, from 0.01332 to 0.09454 for 1–7% noise (Table 10). Error plots show devia-
tions concentrated near boundaries with missing data, yet regularization prevents widespread
error propagation, underscoring the algorithm’s robustness in 3D nonlinear contexts.
Overall, the method attains high accuracy for smooth and moderately non-smooth data
even with compact networks and moderate training sets; flux-based regularization on the
unknown boundary, together with α selection via the discrepancy principle or the L-curve,
is critical for suppressing noise and stabilizing the ill-posed Cauchy problem. For step-
like data or when Cauchy data are limited (e.g., a quarter of the circle), errors increase
as expected yet the solutions remain reasonable, confirming practical applicability. The
approach also scales effectively to curved geometries, 3D, and nonlinear equations, with
numerical solutions closely matching exact ones, demonstrating strong generalization. Taken
together with the tables and figures, these results show that the proposed framework delivers
highly accurate and stable solutions under moderate noise while accommodating diverse
data types, boundary conditions, and geometries without requiring overly complex models
or sophisticated optimization techniques.



Conclusion
We reformulated the Cauchy problem for multi-dimensional nonlinear elliptic equations as a
least squares problem and then stabilized it by Tikhonov regularization method. We solved
the regularized problem in the framework of artificial neural networks, while the regulariza-
tion parameter is chosen by an a posteriori method or the L-curve method. The numerical
results show that the approach is efficient for this severely ill-posed, multi-dimensional and
nonlinear Cauchy problem. Furthermore, it is shown that without the regularization the
method does not work. Thus, ANN alone do not have regularization effect as claimed in
some previous works.
